Medpace Q4 2025 — The Arithmetic Behind the Beat

A second-pass, source-locked forensic read of the 4Q25 earnings presentation
Filing: MEDP 4Q25 Earnings Presentation (Feb 9, 2026) Prepared: April 19, 2026 Scope: 15-page deck; no 10-K available
Financial Lens · Skeptical Read · Non-10-K Source

Bottom Line

Medpace's Q4 2025 headline — revenue +32%, EPS +67% — is a composite of a roughly 10% service business, a 68% surge in reimbursed pass-through revenue, a compressed share count from a $913 million buyback, and a lower tax rate. Management's own 2026 guidance midpoint, $2,805M — below the 4Q25 annualized run-rate of $2,834M, is the most candid admission in the deck that the growth just reported is not the growth ahead.

Executive Summary

The headline overstates organic momentum

Of the $171.9M Q4 revenue increase, $130.2M (≈76%) came from reimbursed out-of-pocket revenue, which is essentially pass-through cost recovery. Direct service revenue — the actual Medpace — grew modestly. Q4 EBITDA margin compressed 230 bps (24.9% → 22.6%), consistent with a higher pass-through mix that carries no margin.

The backlog is burning faster than it is filling

Medpace's quarterly backlog conversion rate ratcheted from 18.3% to 23.6% through 2025 — an unexplained acceleration — while net new business trailed revenue in three of four quarters. Book-to-bill bottomed at 0.90x in 1Q25. The $3.0B backlog declined in Q3 and is now roughly flat YoY even as revenue is guided lower sequentially.

The buyback did the heavy lifting on EPS — and drained the balance sheet

Medpace deployed $912.9M in 2025 buybacks (5.2x the prior year), shrinking the share count from 30.6M to 28.8M. Cash troughed at $46.3M in Q3 before rebuilding to $497M. The capital return was extraordinary in magnitude, and it carried the signal that management saw no better internal use for nearly a billion dollars of cash.

Investigation Threads

Thread 01 · Quality of the Beat

A service business growing at single digits, dressed in pass-through revenue

"$130 million of the $172 million Q4 revenue increase did not meaningfully touch Medpace's P&L as profit. It passed through."

The 68.3% increase in reimbursed out-of-pocket revenue dwarfs the ~8.7% increase in direct service revenue. Pass-through revenue is cost recovery — Medpace bills, then pays, third-party investigators and sites. It carries essentially no margin. The 230 bps Q4 EBITDA margin compression is the tell: if the high-growth component carried service margins, mix shift would not pull blended margin down that far.

The deck never splits revenue growth between direct service and reimbursed OOP components on the same page. Reimbursed OOP is shown as a separate line in the income-statement table, but the narrative framing — "revenue grew 31.8%" — does not acknowledge that three-quarters of that growth is pass-through.

WHY IT MATTERS · Investors who value Medpace on service-revenue multiples risk extrapolating a 32% growth rate into terminal assumptions. The real Medpace service engine grew roughly 10% in 4Q25, at compressing margins. A 2026 sell-side model anchored on 32% top-line growth will miss by a wide margin — not by accident, but because the disclosure invited the error.
Thread 02 · Backlog Mechanics

Faster conversion masking weaker replenishment

"The backlog grew 2.9% year-over-year while revenue grew nearly 24%. Either conversion accelerated, or the backlog is being exhausted."

Medpace's backlog conversion rate rose from 18.3% (1Q25) to 23.6% (4Q25) with no methodological or mix explanation on the page. A higher conversion rate burns backlog faster. Meanwhile book-to-bill fell below 1.0x in 1Q25 and only recovered to 1.28x in Q4 — a single strong quarter in a weak year.

Backlog ended at $3.0 billion, down sequentially in Q3, essentially flat YoY. Net new business for the year was roughly $2.33 billion, below total revenue — meaning the book-to-bill for the full year was below 1.0x by the most meaningful measure.

WHY IT MATTERS · Management is running the backlog faster to hit the revenue number. The 2026 guidance midpoint ($2,805M) is consistent with depleting the accelerated-conversion engine: if 23.6% conversion is the new steady-state, next year's implied new business to hit guidance is north of $2.8B, versus $2.3B booked in 2025. That gap is the real question about the 2026 plan.
Thread 03 · The Buyback Signal

A near-billion-dollar buyback as a statement about opportunity

"Medpace paid out roughly 89% of its FY25 operating cash flow of $1.02 billion as buybacks. That is not capital stewardship; that is a statement about internal reinvestment opportunities."

The $912.9M buyback drew ending cash from $671M (YE24) to a $46M trough in Q3 before rebuilding to $497M. The share count fell from 30.6M to 28.8M. Management delivered EPS growth partly through denominator reduction — a transparent, legitimate, but low-multiple source of earnings growth.

More telling than the size is the implicit priority ranking: management chose to return capital rather than invest in service-line expansion, AI-enabled platform tooling, or M&A. In a CRO industry where competitors are signalling AI/automation investments as competitive differentiators, Medpace's signal is the opposite — "we don't see a better place to put this money."

WHY IT MATTERS · The buyback flatters short-term EPS and suppresses the volatility of any single quarter's miss, but it narrows the balance-sheet cushion against customer cancellations and enrollment risk. With backlog flat, conversion accelerating, and 2026 guided flat, the buyback looks like an exit vote on organic reinvestment — not a vote of confidence in the forward growth algorithm.

Earnings Quality Read

What the headlines conceal

Q4 revenue growth of 31.8% reflects accounting for third-party investigator and site costs, not underlying service growth. Operating income grew 20% and EBITDA grew 20%, both far below the revenue figure — a gap that aligns with a pass-through mix shift, not operational leverage.

What may be better than it looks

Free cash flow of $682M (122% EBITDA conversion) is genuinely strong. Cash generation is tracking earnings. The buyback was executed near share-price lows as the ratio of deployed capital to market-cap movement suggests disciplined timing.

What remains unclear

The conversion-rate acceleration is unexplained. The 1Q25 book-to-bill trough is not addressed. The composition of the 68% reimbursed OOP growth is a black box. The tax-rate swing between Q1 and Q4 is not disclosed.

Follow-Up Questions (For the 10-K)

Ten items, ordered roughly by materiality to the investment case.

  1. Full-year 2025 split of revenue between direct service and reimbursed OOP. YoY growth of each.
  2. What drove the Q4 2025 reimbursed OOP growth of 68.3% — enrollment ramps, site activations, or unit-cost inflation?
  3. Why did backlog conversion rate jump from 18.3% to 23.6% over four quarters? Methodology change?
  4. What was the cancellation dollar volume in 1Q25 that produced the 0.90x book-to-bill?
  5. What share of the $3.0B backlog is tied to customers whose remaining cash runway is under 18 months?
  6. Was the $913M buyback executed via open market or ASR? Any insider selling during the repurchase window?
  7. What drove the 1Q25 effective tax rate of 3% and the 4Q25 rate of 13.8%? Discrete stock-comp benefit?
  8. What is management's 2026 interest income assumption, and how does that factor into EPS guidance midpoint?
  9. What specific AI use cases has Medpace deployed, and where do savings appear — direct service or SG&A?
  10. What share of the NTM $1.9B backlog-to-revenue conversion is derived from the top 10 customers?

Close

Stripped of narrative framing, the 2025 deck tells a simpler story: Medpace's service business grew roughly 10% at compressed margins; pass-through revenue inflated the top line; backlog burned faster than it was replenished; and the company returned nearly a billion dollars to shareholders because it had more cash than it had organic opportunities. Management's 2026 guidance is the most honest line in the deck.
