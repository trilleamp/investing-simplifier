COMPOUNDER IDENTIFICATION FRAMEWORK
Helmer 7 Powers + Porter 5 Forces + Capital Allocation
MASTER PROMPT
Analyze [COMPANY TICKER] as a potential compounding investment using the integrated Helmer-Porter-Capital Allocation framework below. Your goal is to determine whether this company possesses durable competitive advantages AND the ability to reinvest at high returns—the two requirements for long-term compounding.

PART 1: STRUCTURAL ADVANTAGE ASSESSMENT
A. Helmer's Seven Powers (Sources of Durable Advantage)
For each power—Scale Economies, Network Economies, Counter-Positioning, Switching Costs, Branding, Cornered Resource, Process Power:

Verdict: State whether the power is STRONG / MODERATE / WEAK / ABSENT
Evidence: Provide 2-3 specific, verifiable data points that justify your verdict
Economic Quantification: Quantify the margin or return advantage this power provides vs. competitors
Erosion Risks: Top 3 risks that could erode this advantage, with estimated time horizons (e.g., "2-3 years if competitor X achieves Y")
Flywheel Dynamics: Is this power static, strengthening, or eroding over time? Identify any compounding mechanisms.
B. Porter's Five Forces (Competitive Intensity)
For each force—Threat of New Entrants, Supplier Power, Buyer Power, Threat of Substitutes, Industry Rivalry:

Intensity Rating: HIGH / MODERATE / LOW and direction of change (improving/stable/worsening)
Evidence: 2-3 concrete examples with numbers where possible
Margin Impact: How does this force affect gross margin, EBITDA margin, or pricing power?
Structural Change Risks: What could shift this force against the company over 3-5 years?
PART 2: REINVESTMENT CAPACITY ASSESSMENT
Durable advantages without reinvestment opportunities create cash cows, not compounders. For each power identified as STRONG or MODERATE:

Reinvestment Runway: What is the total addressable opportunity for deploying incremental capital at returns exceeding cost of capital? Estimate in dollar terms over 5-10 years.
Incremental ROIC: What returns has the company earned on recent investments? Is incremental ROIC above, at, or below average ROIC?
Capital Deployment Options: Rank the company's reinvestment options: organic growth, M&A, buybacks, dividends, debt paydown. Which creates most value?
Reinvestment Rate: What percentage of earnings is being reinvested vs. returned to shareholders? Is this appropriate given opportunities?
PART 3: MANAGEMENT & CAPITAL ALLOCATION
Evaluate the operators who will execute on the structural advantages:

A. Ownership Alignment
Insider ownership percentage and recent buying/selling patterns
Compensation structure: Are incentives tied to ROIC, FCF per share, or just revenue/EPS?
Board composition and independence
B. Capital Allocation Track Record
M&A history: Calculate ROIC on acquisitions >3 years old. Value-creating or destroying?
Buyback timing: Did they buy back stock when undervalued or at peaks?
Dividend policy: Sustainable payout ratio? Growing appropriately?
Stated vs. revealed priorities: Does management do what they say?
C. Management Quality Indicators (Rochon Framework)
Long-term thinking: Evidence of decisions that sacrifice short-term results for long-term value
Operational excellence: Consistent execution on stated goals
Communication quality: Transparent about problems? Consistent messaging?
Succession planning: Bench strength and transition history
