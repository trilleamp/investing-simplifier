STEP 2: FOUR-DIMENSION SCORING
DIMENSION 1: LIABILITY SHIELD
Weight: 15% of composite

The question: How catastrophic is failure of this software?

The higher the cost of the software being wrong, the harder it is for LLM-native alternatives to displace it. This maps to Bustamante's Regulatory moat — but extends it by asking whether failure creates legal, financial, or human liability that makes switching existentially risky.

Scoring:

Score	Criteria
8–10	Failure causes death, regulatory violation, or massive financial liability. FDA-validated, HIPAA-regulated, SOX-compliant. LLM alternatives are structurally blocked.
5–7	Failure causes significant operational disruption or financial loss but is recoverable. ERP, core banking, insurance claims. LLM alternatives face friction.
2–4	Failure is inconvenient and costly but manageable. CRM, project management, collaboration. LLM alternatives are entering.
0–1	Failure is easily corrected with minimal consequence. Content creation, analytics dashboards, research tools. LLM alternatives are already here.

DIMENSION 2: BUSINESS MODEL STRUCTURE
Weight: 30% of composite

The question: Does the pricing model structurally grow or shrink as AI agents proliferate?

This is the most critical dimension. Seat-based models are structurally impaired because AI agents collapse headcount requirements. Usage/outcome-based models can grow as AI increases activity volume. Bustamante's insight: the real question isn't whether the company has AI features — it's whether the pricing model captures value when AI does the work.

Scoring:

Score	Criteria
8–10	Usage-based or outcome-based pricing that scales with AI-driven activity. AI agents that use the platform generate MORE revenue. API pricing, transaction percentage, outcome fees, consumption models.
5–7	Hybrid: seat + usage. Company has demonstrated willingness to cannibalize seat revenue. AI product line exists with verifiable ARR. New logo growth healthy.
2–4	Primarily seat-based. NRR above 100% but decelerating. Limited usage-based offering. Competing on features vs. AI-native alternatives.
0–1	Pure seat-based with accelerating churn. AI-native alternatives at 20% of price. NRR at or below 100% and falling.
Pre-Screen Cross-Reference: Pull the competition count and disruption stage from the pre-screen. If 20+ AI-native entrants in 24 months, apply downward pressure on this score regardless of current NRR — pricing power degradation typically leads NRR by 4–8 quarters.

DIMENSION 3: PHYSICAL WORLD INTEGRATION
Weight: 15% of composite

The question: Does the software sit in the transaction or in the information layer?

Bustamante's Transaction Embedding moat is the direct mapping. Software embedded in money flows, physical operations, or regulated data collection cannot be disintermediated by LLMs. The LLM sits on top as a better interface, not instead of the software.

Scoring:

Score	Criteria
8–10	Software IS the transaction. Payment processing, loan origination, trade settlement, claims processing, device data ingestion from physical instruments. Turning it off immediately halts revenue for the customer.
5–7	Software coordinates physical operations but isn't the transaction itself. Supply chain orchestration, manufacturing execution, field service management.
2–4	Software manages data about physical operations but is primarily an information layer. Asset management dashboards, maintenance scheduling, IoT analytics.
0–1	Pure information/research/analytics product with no physical world dependency. LLM agents can fully substitute.
Pre-Screen Cross-Reference: Pull Moat 9 (Transaction Embedding) status from the pre-screen. If rated "Information Layer Only," this dimension scores 0–2 regardless of vertical.

DIMENSION 4: NETWORK EFFECTS AND DATA GRAVITY
Weight: 40% of composite

The question: Does the platform get harder to leave as more participants join, and does proprietary data accumulate in ways that cannot be replicated?

This is the heaviest-weighted dimension. It maps to Bustamante's two most durable moats: Proprietary/Private Data (Moat 6) and Network Effects (Moat 8).

Bustamante's core practitioner insight: "If your data genuinely cannot be replicated, LLMs make it MORE valuable, not less." Conversely, companies that organized public data with an intelligence layer are watching that layer commoditize as frontier models replicate it natively.

Scoring:

Score	Criteria
8–10	Proprietary data from exclusive relationships that cannot be scraped or synthesized. Cross-participant network effects where value is a direct function of who else is on the platform. Compounding data flywheel. Examples: real-time trading desk pricing, credit ratings backed by decades of default history, clinical trial data networks.
5–7	Partial proprietary data (some exclusive, some licensed). Network effects present but substitutable. A well-funded competitor could replicate within 3–5 years.
2–4	Primarily organized public data with proprietary intelligence layer on top. Limited network effects. Switching costs are primarily workflow/interface-driven — which the pre-screen has already assessed as eroding.
0–1	Data is public or licensable. No network effects. Intelligence layer is the only differentiator — and that layer is being replicated by frontier models.
Pre-Screen Cross-Reference: Pull Moat 6 (Proprietary Data) and Moat 8 (Network Effects) from the pre-screen. These are the direct inputs to this dimension. If the pre-screen rated Moat 6 as "Overstated" (organized public data rather than truly exclusive data), score D4 at 2–4 maximum.

Bustamante Data Test — apply explicitly: Can this data be obtained, licensed, or synthesized by someone else? - If No: Data moat holds. Likely strengthens in LLM era. Score 7–10. - If Yes, but with significant barriers: Partial moat. Score 4–6. - If Yes, easily: Data moat collapsing. Score 0–3.

Aggregation Theory Risk (Bustamante): If the data moat is weak, assess whether the company is becoming a commodity data supplier to AI agent platforms — where the agent captures the customer relationship and routes to the cheapest data provider. Rate this risk: Low / Medium / High. A High aggregation theory risk caps D4 at 3 regardless of other factors.

Metrics Required: - % of data that is proprietary vs. licensed vs. public (10-K "Data Assets" or "Competitive Strengths" sections) - Evidence of inter-company or cross-participant network effects - Whether company data is available as an MCP server (commoditization signal)

USAGE NOTES
Do not skip the pre-screen upload. Stage 2 is designed to read from Stage 1. Running Stage 2 without the MDI pre-screen defeats the purpose of the two-stage workflow — you will score dimensions without knowing what the moat actually was, which is the most common analytical failure mode.

Flag pre-screen inconsistencies before proceeding. If Stage 1 returned LOW confidence but Stage 2 raw composite comes out Robust, something is wrong. Resolve the discrepancy with evidence before finalizing.

The CPP requires direct quotes. "Management said they're excited about AI" = 0 points. "Agentforce is now $500M ARR growing 330% YoY and we are committed to migrating customers from seat pricing" = 2 points. Hold the standard.

The Valuation Gate is the last line of defense. A company can be genuinely Antifragile and still be uninvestable at 80x revenue. Do not skip it.

Re-run quarterly. The AI landscape is moving fast. Companies that were Robust 6 months ago may now be Fragile. The pre-screen (Stage 1) is the fastest to update — run it first when re-assessing.

The Kill Switch is the most important line in the output. Define it precisely. Without it, the framework becomes a rationalization tool for holding losers.
